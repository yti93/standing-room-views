I have played baseball for most of my life. Youth leagues in Chicago, international school leagues in Abu Dhabi, and rec league adult softball in Portland. I have had great teammates along the way, from familiar faces, to strangers who became friends, and I have learned to trust in them and work in concert towards success. My teammates became my brothers over countless seasons, but my greatest champions in life are my mom and dad. They are first-generation immigrants from Egypt, and as is the case for most Arab-Americans, did not have much of an interest in a sport that was not soccer.

That never stopped them from supporting my love of baseball. They drove me to practices and games, and encouraged me to be great as I told them I was dropping pre-med in college in order to focus on building a career in the sports industry. I would like to talk about one experience in particular that has reminded me time and time again of their selflessness, and has guided me in my 24 years.

Baseball has always been about more than the actual game for me. In 2nd grade, we were asked to do book reports on a historical figure. I decided to do a report on Jackie Robinson by reading Jackie & Me. In summary, 10-year-old Joey Stoshack can time travel by holding rare, historical baseball cards (the series started with Joey finding a T206 Honus Wagner card). He then goes on an adventure in mid-20th century Brooklyn with Jackie himself, realizing what it took to be the first man to break the race barrier in professional sports. As I read Jackie & Me, the inquisitive side of me wanted to find out more about Robinson. As one of the only minorities in my class, I was starting to see the the struggle as an outsider trying to break down socioeconomic barriers through the power of sport.

Jackie Robinson quickly became my favorite historical baseball player, not only for his audacity and instinct on the bases and in the field, but also for the grace and humility he exhibited in a volatile, racially-charged environment. He "looked like he had taken the weight of every African American in the country on his shoulders", a testament to the magnitude of the pressure he felt on his singular journey. The empathy that Branch Rickey and Pee Wee Reese showed Robinson was not lost on me either. Robinson was a shining example in how to let your actions speak louder than words when faced with a hostile situation. His courage and approach is something I have taken to heart throughout my life.

After doing my research, it was time to present my findings in the form of an in-person interview with Robinson. My mother took the day off work, dressed up as a 1950's baseball player, and memorized the entire interview. I felt a little self-conscious that my mom was dressing up as someone she barely knew in order to see me succeed, but the effort she put in on my behalf demonstrated the same type of selflessness seen in Robinson, Rickey, and Reese.

Just as Jackie Robinson was supported by his teammates and general manager at every turn, paying those lessons forward throughout his life - I plan on doing the same. For all the lessons my mom and dad imparted on me about being kind, inclusive, and selfless, I intend on paying it forward throughout my life.
