I first started writing about baseball because I thought it would give me a leg up on applying to internships and jobs within MLB. It may have, it maybe didn't, maybe I didn't put enough effort into applying to those jobs or maybe I didn't want it bad enough compared to other applicants. Or maybe I just wasn't good enough.

Not being good enough is something I've struggled with in my life. While "Try Not To Suck" is a nice little pithy slogan that the Cubs employed a while back, I really take that slogan to heart and soul, much to my detriment. 

Not being good enough to me feels like failure. Starting something new and not being immediately serviceable or good at it feels like failure. Applying to jobs and not getting an email back feels like failure. 

As much as I think I am okay with failure, I really am not.  I think I gave up writing on this not because it wasn't fun (it was), but because I didn't get that job I really wanted. And a lot's changed since then - I've moved towns, moved companies, got married, and moved from wanting a career in baseball to being okay with being a fan of the game. 

Imagine my surprise when I went and rediscovered this archive and re-read some of my old posts. It really feels like I was trying to convince myself that I was going to mature, I'd be okay with failure, and be able to really let it rip and neglected to mention or consider I couldn't do any of that without putting the work and effort into it. 

So we'll see how long this one lasts. I'm in the throes of a more analog approach to life and living. Since the last post (November 2019, almost 7 years), we've had a couple new WS champions (and one obnoxious repeat champion), many rule changes (big fan of the pitch clock and 3 batter RP minimum), and one big fat pandemic. 

I'm starting to write again not to try to get a job, but to get out of a little bit of a rut I've found myself in where I haven't done anything new. The last new things I tried were Muay Thai (quit that after 3 months) and surfing (still get out there occasionally, but not as much as I would like). So I'm getting back into writing. I'm branching out into developing my green thumb and my woodworking chops. 

For someone that prides themselves on being analytical (I know it's BS, but as a lefty it makes sense to me too), I feel a yearning to be more creative and right-brained. It's scary, I'm probably going to fail a lot, and create a lot of things I personally am not happy with the quality of. But that's the point, and I need to keep that in mind - this is more of a reminder for myself and not for anyone else. Even though I'm putting it out on the internet.
