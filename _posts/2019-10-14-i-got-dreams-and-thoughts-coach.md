I had a long weekend full of baseball, football, and showing friends around my town, so I wasn't able to put together a detailed write-up of how glorious the Dodgers losing was, how utterly expected the Yanks/Astros ALCS matchup was but how unexpected the Rays performance was, the Cardinals knocking the Braves out in 4.05888 games...you get the point. Some of my favorite writers summarized the Division Series well, but the best piece of writing that came out last week was Rob Arthur's opus [paywall for BP] on the juiced baseball's disappearing this October like the last summer tan.

Was the ball juiced the last couple of years? Did MLB intentionally "un-juice" the balls this October to prevent an 11-8 slugfest and protect bullpen ERAs? Why now rather than last year?

Here's what I know, and think I know:

- I think the scientific evidence referenced by Arthur is pretty damning and it is pretty plain to see that the drag coefficient has increased on two distinct sets of baseballs - regular season and postseason.

- I do not think it is coincidental that these baseball hijinks began to occur around the same time MLB took on more of an active role in the ownership and management of the manufacturing facilities, which was 2018 according to BizJournal.

- To that end, the obfuscation by MLB is unnecessary in terms of trying to hand-wave the problem away. the Office of the Commissioner knows more than any other entity that money, hard facts, and public opinion matter - and this juiced balls fiasco is affecting their public perception because of the hard work of people like Dr. Meredith Wills and the aforementioned Rob Arthur to bring these facts to the light of day.

- This probably isn't going anyway anytime soon - but I'd rather have a juiced balls fiasco than what the NBA is currently dealing with.

Predictions (2 days late...)

Nationals over Cards in 6 - I had this prediction before the series started, more as a function of how much the Cardinals annoy me than how much I believed in the Nats - but that rotation is here to inspire fear into the hearts of Cardinals' hitters - and it's succeeded so far. We'll see if anyone other than Jose Martinez can consistently hit for St. Louis, otherwise this series is ending in D.C.

Astros over Yanks in 7 - I do have the benefit of writing this after Correa walked off the teams to NYC with a split, but the Astros have to be sweating a little bit. The Yanks have shown their pitching is every bit as dogged as the Astros staff, and the Yanks' bats show no signs of slowing down. I give Houston the edge here because I refuse to predict against a team that gets to throw out JV and the Cole Train 4 times in a potential 7-gamer.

See you all for the World Series. Should be a blast!
