As I've started this blog, one of my fears has been that I wouldn't be able to figure out what to write about. Not that there isn't anything to write about in baseball - namely, it was that I would have a hard time figuring out what was important enough for me to write about. I didn't want to cover something that had been beaten into the ground, LeBron vs. MJ style, nor did I want to write about something that only I found interesting, like the fact that I remember Ronny Cedeño's batting average in 2005 (an even .300).

The middle ground, I've found, has been crowd-sourcing what people want to read about. I've polled my baseball network, ranging from my friends to casual fans to professionals looking to get into front offices like myself, gleaning insights on what they want to read about.

I got suggestions for launch angles, pitcher profiles, and how much MLB spends on baseballs a year. And granted, I want to and will write about all of that in due time! I have appreciated each and every suggestion that has come through as I begin this journey and hope to provide some entertainment to the masses. You've helped me take a look at some data I wouldn't have necessarily been searching for otherwise.

A couple of my friends have asked about Rhys Hoskins, so I thought I'd honor that request and go for it. I'm planning on a longer piece about launch angle (not to browbeat the issue, but it seems like everyone's been talking launch angle these days) and I found a couple of interesting tidbits that I'd like to share with you.

The Statcast Search feature on Baseball Savant is incredibly powerful and allows you to search for virtually any scenario in baseball you can dream of. For me, it's like a beautifully built data playground.

Through May 16th, Rhys Hoskins saw 725 pitches. He's barreled up 15 of those pitches, and 9 for hits - good for a 1.2 percent rate. For reference, Trout and Judge have 15 barreled hits and Stanton has 14 for rates between 1.8 percent and 1.9 percent.

What's interesting is every single barreled hit by Hoskins has come on a fastball that's been low in the zone and/or middle out. He's pulled seven of those nine hits down the left field line, for a total of five doubles and four home runs. Simply put, he's a dead pull fastball hitter - so it follows that he should only be thrown breaking balls to avoid big contact and consequently, big damage, right?

As a young hitter, he should be especially susceptible to breaking balls. However, of his 48 strikeouts, only about half have been on a breaking ball - 26, to be exact. That's not bad - the first piece of evidence he sees fastballs and breaking balls equally well.

Let's take a look at a ball Hoskins barreled up against the Rays on April 13th. This was a long out to center, but I want to dig into this at-bat as an informal predictor of why Rhys doesn't get the Javy Baez low and away breaking ball treatment.

Sergio Romo starts off Hoskins with a low outside changeup that just missed the corner. With the shift on, he's trying to get Rhys to turn over a low pitch and ground into an easy force with two down. He tries to go back to the same corner with what looks like a slow curve, but leaves it a little too in the zone and Hoskins launches it 408 feet, only to see it nestle into Kevin Keiermaier's glove a foot in front of the wall.

It was only a two-pitch at-bat a month ago, but Hoskins looked comfortable waiting on the breaking ball and put a nice swing on a pitch that was inches off where he took the previous pitch. In this at-bat, he demonstrated good pitch recognition and an ability to extend through the zone and go with an outside pitch rather than trying to pull it.

He still might get fooled by a breaking ball here and there, but I personally believe Hoskins is going to keep developing his already decent off-speed approach and make solid contact due to his plate coverage and pitch recognition. That's a scary thought for a guy with 90 major league games under his belt.
