Carl Edwards Jr. has always had plus stuff, but control issues and streakiness have held him back from taking over the closer's job for Joe Maddon's North Side contingent. In year 4 of his career, there have been very encouraging signs that he is putting it all together to dominate.

Firstly, the Cubs' bullpen has been the lone bright spot to start the season, as the rotation has been awful and the hitters as streaky as Wagyu steak. The back end of the bullpen has been outstanding though, and none better than Edwards.

There were two reasons why the Cubs converted Edwards into a reliever – his endurance and his rocket fastball. They believed that he would flourish in shorter, high-leverage situations and groomed him to eventually close games.

As bullpen roles evolved over the last couple years, CJ has gotten stretched out a la Andrew Miller, although not quite to the extent of the rubber-armed Indians fireman. Over his first 10 appearances last year, he went less than one inning three times, with only one appearance of over an inning. To begin 2018, he's pitched at least one inning in every appearance, with one outing of over 3 outs. He's also thrown 50% more pitches and had shorter rest, going 1.6 days of rest in between outings compared to 1.9 days of rest last season.

Let's take a look at some of his statistics too. While his ERA was perfect through 10 innings last year, he's given up an earned run this year. The underlying numbers are almost identical to last year, but there's one major discrepancy. He's been substantially unluckier this year – his BABIP is three times as high as it was last year, suggesting that he'll normalize throughout the course of the season and improve on his luck. BABIP is prone to fluctuations in the early going and Edwards' ability to induce weak contact will help bring it down.

As previously mentioned, Edwards has some of the nastiest stuff in the game, with a high spin-rate fastball and a devastating curve that work off each other. He's been able to locate his fastball well this year, and an almost 1mph drop in his curve has allowed him to pick his spots and throw it with increased accuracy. In the past, hitters have waited out his curveball and sat on his fastball, but they are unable to do so now that he's throwing the curve consistently for strikes. With his rising fastball exhibiting a natural cut, it's a deadly combo that has allowed him to double his strikeouts in the early going.

He's had some trouble coming in with runners on base, allowing 75% of them to score in the early going vs. stranding all 5 inherited runners last year in his first 10 appearances. Maddon trusts CJ more than any reliever in his bullpen, and goes to him often, even during one of his prolonged cold streaks in tense situations to try and jumpstart him. Edwards has spoken about how important confidence and feel is to him, and sometimes his commands deserts him Edwards is good for one or two outings every month where he can't find the zone.

His early progress looks encouraging as he appears to be stronger and more comfortable pitching whenever Maddon needs him. He looks to have turned a corner regarding his control issues and use of his curveball, as he looks to turn in another strong season. Expect 70 innings with an ERA under 2.00 when it's all said and done. Brandon Morrow is the closer for the next two years, but don't be surprised if the Cubs elect to go in-house to fill their closer role in 2020.
