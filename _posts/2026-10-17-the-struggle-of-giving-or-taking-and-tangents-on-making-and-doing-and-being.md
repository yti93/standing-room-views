In terms of giving and taking, I am a taker. I consume voraciously. I read. I scroll. I eat, and I drink. I pilfer opinions in the name of triangulating my own viewpoints, and pass them off as original. I burn tokens in the name of productivity, and I am putting all of these things into my body and mind, and taking from the world, one screen at a time without understanding/reflecting/synthesizing. 

This is what this new world paradigm does to us - forces us to consume and consume and consume, lest we miss out, without providing time for us to catch our breath for fear of missing out.

Humanity is meant to give, and historically has been meant to - to provide back into the world, to take care of others, to balance out what we take with what we give. This is something I have struggled with recently. I feel this with everything I consume, that it was meant for me, for someone, to find a lesson worth learning in, or some nugget of information.

I have been working on putting back out into the world rather than taking it all in for myself recently, and I'm taking a lot of baby steps, but it still feels gratifying to recognize and work on it bit by bit. 

I'm still reading a lot, but I am trying to sit and reflect on each piece I read - meaning I sit and think on what it means, and actually write down my interpretation. I don't mean for it to be consumed even, or reviewed, or liked - I just want to feel like I'm restoring my own balance to the world. 

I am planting and nursing vegetables in the backyard of our rental house. I am working on building things, tiny crude simple things, out of wood, to make something bigger out of smaller somethings. I am giving these things to my friends, in the hopes that it provides a bit of joy, or is useful, or is something. Is this inherently selfish, that I'm writing about things I'm doing for others? Well, probably - but it's my way of accountability.

A couple of weeks ago, a stray cat brought her tiny little kittens into our yard, and I felt such a pull to help, and was partially heartbroken when I could not approach them without mother cat becoming skittish (which I have since learned is to be expected). I would leave out cat food and come back to an empty container. 

One day I was leaving on an errand and saw one of the three kittens motionless in the middle of our quiet street and it broke me. As my wife and a friend consoled me, they reminded me this is the way of life. I appreciate their sentiment but at the time I felt they were being callous, although I had failed to explain to them just how much this little feline family had made an impact on my heart in a week. Mom disappeared too, and this interaction, however impactful, was fleeting and had reached its conclusion.

Mama cat came back to the yard a week later, and I have set food out for her a couple of times, and she's let me get a little closer, although she's still a bit skittish. 

I also recently chatted with a friend who's also been writing recently and just putting stuff out into the world and doing some reflection on the human condition. That's also been inspiring to see someone else take that leap of faith and attach something to themselves so publicly. 

It's hard! We are (and have been) conditioned to feel like what we put out into the universe needs not only to be our best effort, but needs to be polished, or can be monetized/should get views, or should be used to get a higher paying job. Why put anything out into the world for free, when you can stand to gain from it? 

As an aside: maybe these feelings and this opinion of mine is because I spend a lot of time in the technology world and am terminally online, but that's probably a conversation for a different time. It is just a requirement at this current moment in my life to be online (whether you agree or not is a different story as well).

What I'm learning is that to keep doing what you can, for yourself and for others, no matter how big or how small takes work. Rather than the easy route of just taking and mindlessly consuming from the world, recognizing being there for others and naming it is doable. You can put stuff out into the world, give others help, support, and little things that keep your motor running. You keep showing up, you keep putting yourself into the world, you keep giving, rather than settle on taking from the world, and maybe life feels a little more manageable.
