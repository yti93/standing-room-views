I don't know what to write about now that the season is over.

Not that there isn't any lack of content, but there's a lot to process. The Nationals won an incredibly unlikely title on the strength of their starting pitching and a relentless late-inning approach, so much so that not even the likes of the Astros could topple them at the end.

The season kicked off in Japan 7 months ago, with a homecoming fit for a King - not Hernandez, but Ichiro, possibly the most famous international baseball player of all-time. That's saying something. The Mariners started off hot, and away we went with the 2019 edition of the regular season.

We saw strikeout #3,000 for CC and for JV, two of the best pitchers of my lifetime. Two remarkable careers with different paths converged on one of the pantheon pitcher milestones - CC with a remarkably steady career and a decade(!) in pinstripes was all class in his final season, and JV continuing his late career surge towards the Hall of Fame with a capstone season. Somehow, we might witness the best season in JV's lifetime as a footnote to Gerrit Cole's historic showing, piling up almost 400 Ks over 7 grueling months with a 4-month winning streak.

We thought we had two mortal locks for Rookie of the Year in Juniors Guerrero and Tatis, only for them to be overshadowed by two lesser-known thumpers who took full advantage of batter-friendly conditions (and equipment) all season. While Pete Alonso mashed from opening day, set a new rookie record for dingers in the final series of the season, and delivered plenty of highlights in between, Yordan Alvarez took the league by storm and lengthened that righty-dominant Astros lineup with his left-handed hammer swing. Both rookies look like middle-of-the-lineup mainstays for the considerable future.

Speaking of thumpers, everyone around the league turned into the Great Bambino it seemed. This is purely a qualitative review, but if I were to dig into the data, we'd see some extreme trends. This was the year of the record, as the previous team home run record was smashed - by a handful of teams! Strikeouts rose in baseball (again) as teams swung for the fences - literally and figuratively. Teddy Roosevelt was thinking of career Quadruple-A players' hopes and dreams when he advised them to speak softly and carry a big stick in 2019.

We witnessed ugly storylines of hate and rancor, furthering the argument that we as fans cannot and should not stick to sports. For many of us, sports are our lifeline to society, the way we relate to others, foster packs and tribes, and get through the drudge that is a Wednesday 8:30AM meeting. Baseball hit bingo on hot button societal issues this year: racism, domestic violence, misogyny, the Supreme Court (who paid for your Nats season tickets, Kavanaugh?) and the opioid epidemic. Staunchly rooted in the past for better or for worse, baseball needs to modernize and progress on these issues - the platitudes are ineffective and indicative of a static view.

The playoffs were a treat - as the Cubbies failed to make it this season, I could enjoy October baseball for the first time in 4 years. As hard as I try to spin it, 2016 was an ulcer-inducing roller coaster with the highest of highs ephemerally attained - not a rollicking good time as it may seem.

The Nationals stunned everyone. They stunned me. If you follow baseball, you already know the ESPN talking points: Washington won 19 of their first 50 games, then won 2 out of every 3 games going forward. Their manager's ticker was on the fritz, yet still in better shape than their bullpen. They lost The Chosen One to a division rival, then replaced him with the next Great Bambino, a crotch-grabbing minor up until the World Series. Contrary to popular belief, the Toronto Raptors were not the first team to "load manage" a San Diego State Aztec alum and star - yet the Nat's "Process" finally bore the fruits of its labor as Stephen Strasburg had an all-time postseason. Baby Shark, race car driving, letting the Old Men Play, miracle comebacks - even though this was a low-watched World Series by casual standards, this Nationals team had something for everyone.

We're going to see some rule changes next year. How will the addition of another active player to the Major League roster affect makeup? How will a 3-batter minimum affect one of the great sports abbreviations and specializations of all-time - the LOOGY (Left Handed One Out GuY)? Will we see a un-de-not-juiced ball?

As I sit here today, I wonder who we'll be talking about this time next year as well. There are rookies sure to make a splash, unheralded pitchers breaking out (Liam Hendriks, anyone?), another scintillating Mike Trout season (hopefully free of injury), the return of Ohtani - SP, the potential new landing spots for Cole and Rendon with Betts and Lindor possibly swapping zip codes out...the list goes on and on.

144 days until Opening Day.
