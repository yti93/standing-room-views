I've been very fortunate to attend a lot of baseball games in my lifetime. Granted, not as many as I would like, but that's the struggle, isn't it? 162 games for each team, and never enough time to watch as much baseball as I would like.

One of the best parts of being a baseball fan is rehashing seminal moments in your fandom, where you can trace your love of baseball back to a particular game. It's particularly gratifying to point to a game and say, "That's how I came to love the sacrifice bunt", or "screw a walk-off, the loudest I've ever heard a stadium was when the batter charged the mound."

Baseball means a lot to me. I've gone with family and friends, strengthened relationships over ballpark dogs and dueling fandoms, and learned more about myself and who I'm with during pitching changes.

I'd like to briefly discuss three of my favorite games I've ever attended. These three games may or may not be my top 3 of all time - I haven't decided yet, but they definitely are the ones I talk about the most often whenever someone asks about the most memorable baseball experiences.

Honorable mentions include: Whenever I see the Cubs play in a new ballpark, the Hillsboro Hops games I've had the pleasure of attending since I moved to Portland, my first ever game (Cubs-Brewers, Sunday April 11th, 2004 - my mom was too cold, and we left after the 9th - only to listen to the Cubs walk it off from the car), and the Cubs NLDS Game 3 in 2007. This will be a three-part series, and part one is below.

Jake Arrieta's No-Hitter - August 30th, 2015.

![the Beard and I at Second City Comedy Club, May 2016.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/2018-05-10-an-oral-history-of-my-favorite-baseball-games-part-i/01.jpg)
*the Beard and I at Second City Comedy Club, May 2016.*

The Cubs got no-hit by Cole Hamels and the Phillies right after the All-Star Break in 2015, were hovering around .500, and the postseason didn't look like a possibility with the Pirates and Cardinals running away with the best two records in baseball at that point. It all changed when Kris Bryant walked off the Rockies shortly after the no-no, and Jake Arrieta began his incredible stretch of dominance. To this date, I've never witnessed a pitcher so completely in his element. Every fastball had late movement, and his breaking stuff was simply unhittable. No one could touch him, and it was a guaranteed win for the Cubs whenever Arrieta took the bump that second half.

I was going to school in Los Angeles at the time, and my sister surprised me with great tickets to the middle game of the Cubs-Dodgers series in late August. I realized in mid-August that Arrieta was lined up to pitch the series finale, so I bugged my roommates to buy tickets to the following night's game with me, promising them that I would entertain them by freaking out about Arrieta. I was expecting a dominant performance, but as was the case that magical summer, Arrieta exceeded even the loftiest of expectations.

Having watched Kershaw the day before, I was spoiled with great pitching. We got the park shortly before first pitch, and grabbed food and drinks before settling into our seats high up behind home plate in section 1 at Dodger Stadium. Even from that vantage point, you could see Arrieta had his stuff moving all over the zone from the early innings. Things were going according to plan.

![The View from Section 1.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/2018-05-10-an-oral-history-of-my-favorite-baseball-games-part-i/02.jpg)
*The View from Section 1.*

Memories are tricky to put down on paper. People tend to romanticize, leaving out the mundane in favor of the unusual. The fact of the matter was, Arrieta was being his usual second-half self, so much so that I never really looked at the scoreboard until the 5th or 6th inning. Jake had taken no-hitters into the 5th inning every other start, or so it seemed. It was then that I realized there was a prominent "0" under the Dodgers' Hit column, and my mind flirted with the possibility of history, as one tends to do around that stage of a potential no-no. I knew he had to go through the lineup at least once more, so I tried to keep a little calm.

That plan went out the door when Peter started asking me if he was going to throw a no-hitter every minute. Two of my friends were relative baseball novices, but at least Pete knew enough about baseball to follow along. I began to immediately regret my decision to ask them to accompany me as Mike and Chris joined in on, what my guess is, the world record for mentioning the phrase "no-hitter" as many times as possible during an in-progress no-no.

Between me telling my friends to shut up and damning myself for bringing the jinx to the ballpark, Jake the Snake kept mowing down hitters left and right. There were great defensive plays by Addison Russell, Starlin Castro, and others, with each dive, snare, and throw managing to guide my leaping heart back into place.

It was around the 8th inning that Mike and Chris left their seats to find the bathroom and try and find a last call somewhere. Why they did so, I'll never know. Pete and I sat there and by then, I was full-on yelling at him to stop jinxing it - I can't print what I called him multiple times (sorry mom). At this point I had tuned out the top half of the innings in favor of reserving my energy for the last six outs.

Arrieta struck out the side in the 9th, and re-watching my shaky home video of the last out truly still is an out of body experience. As soon as Utley swung through strike three, I started screaming and high-fiving all the Cubs fans around me. Peter shows up in the video for a flash, looking embarrassed and happy for me at the same time. Funnily enough, that's when the memories started to come back to me as I couldn't believe I witnessed history.

![Elation and Disbelief.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/2018-05-10-an-oral-history-of-my-favorite-baseball-games-part-i/03.jpg)
*Elation and Disbelief.*

To date, that's the most thrilling Cubs game I've ever seen *in person*. I hope you enjoyed reading a little bit about what it was like.
